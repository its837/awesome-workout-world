# USB debugging still unlocks Peloton tablets

People take over Peloton tablets in November 2026 exactly as they did in 2020: they switch on Android's Developer options, enable USB debugging, and use `adb` from a computer to install apps and choose a new home screen. No root, exploit, bootloader unlock or custom ROM exists for current firmware, and OpenPelo, the tool most guides point to, is only a graphical wrapper around those stock commands. The route is confirmed first-hand between September 2025 and November 2026 on original-Bike tablets running Android 11, on the Android 10 tablet shared by the Bike+ and the 2021 Tread, and on the newest Android 13 tablet. What has stopped working is everything around it: the hidden app list, the wireless-debugging toggle on many units, enabling launcher components from ADB on Android 10 and later, the 2021 fastboot root and, most relevant to a household with no membership, installs on some Android 7.0 tablets (`keys mismatch. install denied`) and Netflix left unmodified (closed by Peloton's `com.onepeloton.systempluginui`). Every part of the family's app except two has a working open-source precedent: OpenRide (home-screen app, settings, self-update, bike sensors), the Peloton-Tread "Watch" launcher (a streaming-app grid as HOME on a Tread), and grupetto plus a small overlay project (read-only treadmill speed and incline); the WebView game and over-the-air web-content updates are new ground on this hardware. Live data comes from one unguarded local Android service, `com.onepeloton.affernetservice`, which any installed app can bind: on bikes it streams cadence, power and resistance through `IV1Interface` regardless of subscription state, and on the Tread it reports belt speed and incline through two getter transactions (17 and 18) on `ITreadInterface` whenever the machine is powered, even locked. The two machines call for opposite postures: on the Bike the family can replace the home screen and, if Peloton's software interferes, disable individual Peloton packages reversibly, while on the Tread every Peloton package must stay enabled, because the sensor service locks the belt within five seconds when Peloton's own clients disappear and the only published way around that is to send belt-control commands. A Tread+ is a different platform with no published sensor protocol, so there the app can at best be a launcher without live data. The first session with each tablet should consist of read-only checks (model, Android build, `peloton_platform`, sensor-service version, WebView version, free storage), because those answers decide which of the paths below applies.

## Every method that works in 2026 is plain developer mode and ADB

Almost every walkthrough published between 2024 and 2026 funnels into one toolchain: open Android's Settings on the tablet, tap Build number seven times, switch on USB debugging, plug in a computer, install apps with `adb install`, then pick a new launcher. **OpenPelo**, the tool most videos and guides point to, is a free desktop app (first released 24 December 2024, **v1.0.84 on 1 November 2026**, MIT-licensed) written by the same person who runs the "netwalkergoogle" YouTube channel, so the video series and the repository are one voice, not independent confirmation ([OpenPelo](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo), [releases](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/releases), [October 2026 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y2TonDgQtys)). Read at source level, OpenPelo is a Flutter program whose entire device-facing logic is two files that shell out to a bundled copy of Google's `adb` and push a bundled screen-mirroring server. It contains no table of Peloton models: "is this a Peloton?" is a single pattern match on the manufacturer string, and no feature is gated on the answer ([adb_service.dart](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/blob/86de70f/lib/services/adb_service.dart), [device_model.dart](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/blob/86de70f/lib/models/device_model.dart)). Its command set is small and standard: `adb install -r -d -g -t` (dropping flags one at a time when an old Android rejects them), `cmd package set-home-activity <component>`, a few `settings put` calls, `adb tcpip 5555`, `adb uninstall` with a `pm uninstall --user 0` fallback, and one `pm disable-user --user 0 com.onepeloton.systempluginui`. It writes no system properties, never reboots the tablet and needs no root; the maintainer states plainly that it contains no exploits and uses only the generic developer mode ([adb_service.dart](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/blob/86de70f/lib/services/adb_service.dart), [issue #29](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/29)). A family that prefers scripts can therefore reproduce everything OpenPelo does with plain `adb`.

Independent first-hand reports confirm the route without the tool. In November 2025 an owner of an unsubscribed first-generation Bike limited to Just Ride installed a launcher and apps from a Linux laptop using nothing but the standard toggles and `adb install` ([de Groot](https://ciarandegroot.com/posts/peloton-sideloading/)). In October 2026 three different tablets accepted installs over USB: an original-Bike tablet `PLTN-RB1VQ` on Android 11 build `RQ.260424.A`, a Bike+ tablet `PLTN-TTR01` on Android 10 build `QT.231113.B`, and the newest `g700` tablet on Android 13 build `TA.260520.H` ([OpenRide issue #47](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/47), [OpenRide issue #63](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/63)). The older guides (DC Rainmaker's 2020 article, the 2022 Connect The Watts video with about 221,000 views, a December 2022 Tread+ gist) describe the same skeleton, but their specifics, such as the hidden app list and particular Nova Launcher versions, are obsolete ([DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2020/06/zwift-trainerroad-peloton.html), [Connect The Watts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O8Vq-JK1um8), [Tread+ gist](https://gist.github.com/wadewegner/1d6d1bdaac7f50ae170e18fb64449a30)). Vendor material from FitSwitch, which sells a $139.99 plug-in box, is current but self-interested, and its own install page still requires Build number ×7 and USB debugging, so the box depends on the same developer options as every other method ([FitSwitch comparison](https://fitswitch.io/blogs/learn/openpelo-vs-fitswitch), [FitSwitch installation](https://fitswitch.io/pages/bike-installation)). One 2025 press article says a membership is mandatory for sideloading; the first-hand reports above show that is not true on every unit ([BGR](https://www.bgr.com/1959593/how-to-watch-netflix-peloton-sideload/)).

Nothing deeper than ADB-level control is proven on current firmware. The one public root, McAfee's 2021 `fastboot boot` trick on the Bike+ and Tread, was patched in June 2021 ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/peloton-bike-plus-vulnerability-allowed-complete-takeover-of-devices/)). The only bootloader dump in the record, from a commercial unit in October 2026, shows a locked, fused device whose unlock ability reads 0 ([OpenPelo issue #53](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/53)). No root for the original Bike and no custom ROM for any Peloton tablet was found, and OpenPelo's maintainer explicitly declines to recommend Shizuku-based removal of Peloton services because it can leave a tablet unusable ([OpenPelo issue #24](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/24)). The remaining "deep" options go around the tablet instead: tapping the original Bike's serial line (19,200 bps over a TRRS jack, the PeloMon project) or replacing a dead first-generation tablet with a TV ([PeloMon](https://ihaque.org/posts/2020/10/15/pelomon-part-i-decoding-peloton/), [smart-TV video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qRCMldyr-lk)). None of that is needed for the family's design.

The pattern behind the table matters more than any single row. Peloton has never removed Developer options or USB debugging across the fleet, but since 2021 it has repeatedly tightened what happens around them: drawing overlays over install and authorization dialogs, re-registering its own launcher as the default after an update (April 2021), gating installs on activation state, killing streaming apps when no membership is present (late 2025), and covering third-party home screens (2026) ([DC Rainmaker comments](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2020/06/zwift-trainerroad-peloton.html), [OpenPelo #24](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/24), [OpenRide #63](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/63)). A factory reset wipes user data and updated Peloton apps but does not roll back the system image, which is consistent with both the "reset, then install before the next update" successes and the "still missing after a reset" failures; this explanation is an inference from the reports, not something any source states. Nobody has documented a durable way to block Peloton's updates other than keeping the tablet offline, which a streaming-and-OTA home screen cannot do, so the design has to tolerate Peloton components changing underneath it ([PREVENTING-UPDATES.md](https://github.com/nate-santos/peloton-without-subscription-guide/blob/main/PREVENTING-UPDATES.md), [OpenRide INSTALL.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md)).

## Four open projects already contain each part of the family's app

No single project matches the family's design, but four together cover it. The table summarises what each one is and what is worth taking from it; the paragraphs after it turn that into a blueprint.

### The launcher: a HOME activity that always leaves a way back

OpenRide and the Tread launcher register as HOME in two different ways, and the difference decides how the family installs. OpenRide puts the `MAIN` + `HOME` + `DEFAULT` filter on an `<activity-alias>` that ships disabled, turns it on from a settings row with `PackageManager.setComponentEnabledSetting`, then asks Android for the HOME role and falls back to the system's home-app settings screen; it refuses to turn itself off when no other home app is enabled, because the tablet would then boot into Android's fallback home and hang ([AndroidManifest.xml](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml), [HomeScreenLauncher.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/ui/profile/HomeScreenLauncher.kt)). The Tread launcher puts the filter directly on a `singleTask` activity and is set once with `adb shell cmd package set-home-activity`, after saving the previous home component for the revert script ([Peloton-Tread manifest](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/app/AndroidManifest.xml), [apply.sh](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/app/apply.sh)). For an app that is always meant to be the home screen, **the enabled-from-install HOME filter on a `singleTask` activity is the better fit**, because ADB can set it on Android 10 and later, where the shell is forbidden from enabling a disabled component; adding OpenRide's in-app HOME-role row gives the family a way to switch without a computer. `singleTask` is not optional: without it, pressing Home after opening the app from its icon started a second copy in a new task ([OpenRide #47](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/47), [AndroidManifest.xml](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml)). No boot receiver is needed to come back after a reboot, since being the default home is what starts the activity; the stock Peloton launcher must stay installed as the fallback ([OpenRide INSTALL.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md)).

The settings screen needs little that is Peloton-specific. OpenRide reaches Android's Wi-Fi and top-level Settings with plain `Settings.ACTION_WIFI_SETTINGS` and `Settings.ACTION_SETTINGS` intents, which matters because once a custom app is home the stock Settings icon is no longer one tap away; Bluetooth headphones need only pairing in Android Settings, after which audio follows them automatically ([SettingsShortcuts.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/ui/profile/SettingsShortcuts.kt), [OpenRide INSTALL.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md)). The launcher should not depend on changing wallpaper: Peloton's firmware refuses it with an administrator restriction ([OpenPelo #70](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/70)).

### The WebView game is the one part with no precedent

OpenRide's games are a Godot 4.7.2 project packed inside the APK, and its only WebView is a YouTube class player, so no Peloton project hosts a game in a WebView or refreshes web content over the air ([GameHostActivity.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/games/GameHostActivity.kt), [docs/GAMES.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/GAMES.md)). What does transfer is the measured environment. On the Android 11 original-Bike tablet every app window is **1920×1008 pixels (1280×672 dp at density 1.5)**, because a Peloton component reserves the bottom strip and no immersive flag reclaims it; the working rule is to keep anything important out of the bottom 48 dp ([OpenRide #57](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/57), [MAIN_UI_REFRESH.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/MAIN_UI_REFRESH.md)). The system WebView is **Chromium 127 on the Bike+ tablet but was stuck at 91 on one MT8173 original-Bike tablet running Android 11**, so the game should be built and tested against a Chromium 91 feature set ([DEVICE.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/DEVICE.md), [OpenRide #73](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/73)). That tablet has a PowerVR GX6250 GPU and about 2 GB of RAM; the project's rendering rule is flat surfaces with no blur, shadows or full-screen gradients, and its numbers are for Godot, not for WebView canvas, which nobody has measured ([MAIN_UI_REFRESH.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/MAIN_UI_REFRESH.md)). Any video inside the WebView should be H.264: left alone, YouTube chose VP9 at 1080p60 and the tablet dropped about 40% of frames, against about 2% for H.264 at 720p60 ([OpenRide PR #89](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/pull/89)).

Three behavioural details save debugging time. Media playback in a WebView makes the system bars reappear without any focus change, so immersive mode must be re-applied from an insets listener as well as on focus ([MainActivity.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/MainActivity.kt)). Android hands audio to one app at a time, and a WebView stays out of the way only if its media was silent from the first frame, which matters if the game plays music while Spotify is running ([YouTubeEmbed.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/ui/classes/YouTubeEmbed.kt)). The tablet's global log level is W, so informational logs vanish unless raised per tag over ADB after each boot ([docs/GAMES.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/GAMES.md)). For the native-to-game link, OpenRide passes its engine a small numeric frame that the game polls every frame (cadence, power, resistance, speed, a sensors-ok flag) plus JSON strings for events; that shape maps directly onto a JavaScript interface in a WebView ([OpenRideBridgePlugin.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/games/bridge/OpenRideBridgePlugin.kt)). Finally, the display times out after 30 minutes on the bike and Peloton's software then starts its own setup activity in a new task; a background task that had been excluded from recents was destroyed at that moment, so nothing holding game state should be marked that way, and the screen should be kept on only during an active ride ([docs/GAMES.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/GAMES.md)).

### Tiles launch phone builds of streaming apps, and YouTube is the awkward one

A tile is a `getLaunchIntentForPackage` call with `FLAG_ACTIVITY_NEW_TASK`, which is how OpenRide opens the rider's music app and how the Tread launcher opens everything; with a modern target SDK the manifest also needs a `<queries>` entry for launcher activities ([MusicApps.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/core/music/MusicApps.kt), [OpenRide PR #62](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/pull/62)). The apps must be touch phone/tablet builds: Android TV packages refuse to install because the tablet does not report the leanback feature ([Peloton-Tread AGENTS.md](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/AGENTS.md)). The evidence per tile is uneven. **Netflix** is the best supported: it needs no Google Play Services and ran throughout on a 2021 Tread tablet in November 2026 (on a machine whose app-closing Peloton package had been disabled), and Peloton pre-installs a Netflix package on bikes ([Peloton-Tread README](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/README.md), [OpenPelo #24](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/24)). **Hulu** 6.34.0 from Aurora Store installs and reaches a completed login on an Android 11 original-Bike tablet, but one user hits a location-services error after login and nobody has confirmed playback ([OpenPelo #67](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/67)). **Spotify** is listed as tried on the bike with OpenRide's media controls, with no version or install detail ([OpenRide README](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/README.md)). The official **YouTube** app is the worst fit: on the Tread it played only signed out, and only with Google's services framework installed before it and Play services running; YouTube and Play services together occupy roughly 540 MB of a data partition that is about 4 GB in total ([Peloton-Tread AGENTS.md](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/AGENTS.md), [Peloton-Tread README](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/README.md)). The proven alternative is OpenRide's: the YouTube IFrame player inside the app's own WebView, loaded under an https base URL with a script that hides VP9 and AV1 support, which works on both the Bike+ and the original Bike ([YouTubeEmbed.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/ui/classes/YouTubeEmbed.kt)). A signed-in YouTube inside that WebView is not achievable, because Google blocks its sign-in in embedded WebViews ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/en/upcoming-security-changes-to-googles-oauth-20-authorization-endpoint-in-embedded-webviews/)).

Two cross-cutting facts shape the tiles. Google Play Services cannot be made healthy on these locked builds, and microG needs signature spoofing the firmware lacks, so the community's store is Aurora Store, with its device-spoof setting on a Pixel Tablet profile when an app is reported as unsupported ([Peloton-Tread AGENTS.md](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/AGENTS.md), [OpenPelo #62](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/62)). And **no source reports the Widevine level or the resolution Netflix actually delivers on any Peloton tablet**; Netflix itself documents that an L3 device is limited to standard definition and shows the level in the app's playback-specification screen, so each unit has to be checked there ([Netflix help](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/23939)).

### Getting home reliably takes two layers

Three mechanisms are individually proven on Peloton hardware, and none is sufficient alone. Being the default home means the on-screen home control drawn by Peloton's software returns to the launcher; OpenRide's riders rely on exactly that, and the bike has no recents button ([OpenRide PR #62](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/pull/62)). A draw-over-apps window from a foreground service is what grupetto uses for its metrics and what the Tread launcher uses for a bar that sits on top of a show without taking its touches; the permission can be granted during setup with `adb shell appops set <package> SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW allow`, and the service has to be restarted at boot ([apply.sh](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/app/apply.sh), [CoverService.java](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/app/src/com/treadcover/CoverService.java), [grupetto README](https://github.com/doudar/grupetto/blob/develop/README.md)). An accessibility service runs on an Android 11 original Bike in the Touch Guard project, including detection of a long press on Volume Down ([Touch Guard](https://github.com/murface/touch-guard/blob/main/docs/INSTALLATION.md)). The practical layering is default home plus a small always-on-top Home pill, with the accessibility route held back as an optional extra on the Bike. Two caveats apply. No Peloton project actually ships a floating Home button, so this is assembled from adjacent proven pieces. And Peloton's own secure overlay sits above any app overlay, while one Tread owner found that Peloton's own speed-and-incline overlay vanishes after 10 to 15 minutes outside the Peloton app, so the lifetime of a long-running overlay on this firmware should be tested, not assumed ([OpenRide #63](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/63), [OpenPelo #46](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/46)).

### Updates split into a rare APK and a frequent web bundle

For the app itself, OpenRide's pattern is the only one with a Peloton track record: on launch it reads the latest GitHub release, compares an integer in the asset filename with its own version code, downloads the APK to its cache over https, and hands it to Android's installer with an `ACTION_VIEW` intent on a `FileProvider` URI; the rider taps Check, Download and Install separately and grants "install unknown apps" once ([UpdateRepository.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/core/update/UpdateRepository.kt), [UpdateChecker.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/core/update/UpdateChecker.kt), [OpenRide INSTALL.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md)). Silent updates are not available: Android's no-user-action install option was added in API level 31, above the Android 10 and 11 these tablets run ([PackageInstaller.SessionParams](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/content/pm/PackageInstaller.SessionParams#setRequireUserAction(int))). Every update must be signed with the key used for the first install, or the only way forward is an uninstall that wipes the app's data ([OpenRide INSTALL.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md), [OpenPelo README](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/blob/86de70f/README.md)). One honest gap: no independent rider report confirms that the installer's confirmation screen completes on Peloton firmware when triggered from inside an app; the indirect evidence is that Aurora Store installs apps on an Android 11 tablet ([OpenPelo #67](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/67)).

Game content should not travel through the installer at all. No Peloton project live-updates a web bundle, so the recipe is generic Android practice: the launcher fetches a small manifest (version, URL, SHA-256, signature, minimum app version), verifies the signature against a key baked into the APK, unpacks into a versioned folder in private storage, switches a pointer, and promotes the new bundle only after the page reports itself ready, otherwise reverting to the previous one, with the bundle shipped inside the APK as the last resort. That rollback pattern is the one live-update frameworks for hybrid apps use, and `WebViewAssetLoader` is Android's supported way to serve such a folder to a WebView under an https origin without a local web server ([Capgo](https://capgo.app/blog/cordova-hot-code-push-alternative/), [WebViewAssetLoader](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/webkit/WebViewAssetLoader)). On these tablets the recipe needs three local rules: gate bundles on the WebView version because of the Chromium 91 unit, check free space first because the data partition is small, and never swap a bundle during a ride.

## The original Bike is the proven path, once its tablet generation is known

### First connection: the same read-only checks on both tablets

Before anything is installed or disabled, each tablet should answer the questions below. All of these commands only read state; several are the ones the projects themselves use, and the rest are standard Android tooling ([Peloton-Tread AGENTS.md](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/AGENTS.md), [grupetto Peloton.kt](https://github.com/doudar/grupetto/blob/af9eb90c53/app/src/main/java/com/spop/poverlay/util/Peloton.kt#L39-L72), [OpenRide INSTALL.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/INSTALL.md)).

### Three different tablets hide behind "original Bike"

The phrase "older original Bike" covers three situations with very different prospects. The first-generation **Quartz** tablet (sold 2013 to 2016, Android 4.1.1, serial numbers beginning 7201, 015, 014 or 0B0) lost class access on 19 June 2024; OpenPelo now retries installs without the flags that tablet rejects (no owner has confirmed the result), but Google-dependent apps fail, YouTube would not play in its Firefox, and no project documents reading its sensors on the tablet, so the family's design should be treated as out of scope for it ([Pelo Buddy](https://www.pelobuddy.com/quartz-tablet-support/), [OpenPelo #39](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/39), [OpenPelo #2](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/2)). Tablets still on **Android 7.0** (builds such as `NRZ63B-120`) are where the install gate bites, where current launchers that need Android 8 fail, and where nothing in the record says which sensor service is present ([OpenPelo #55](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/55), [OpenPelo #11](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/11)). Tablets on **Android 11** (`PLTN-RB1VO` on `RO.250111.A`, `PLTN-RB1VQ` on `RQ.260424.A`) are the well-trodden path: MediaTek MT8173, 2 GB RAM, a Micro-USB data port on the back, working installs and a verified sensor interface ([OpenPelo #14](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/14), [OpenRide #47](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/47), [wellfitinsider](https://wellfitinsider.com/peloton/peloton-bike-generations/)). OpenRide adds that some of these bikes sit on an Android 10 firmware and cannot take an update, which is why its minimum SDK is 29; if the family's Bike turns out to be on Android 7.0, their app needs a lower minimum than any of the reference projects use ([DEVICE.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/DEVICE.md)).

### Cadence, resistance and power come from six numbers

On a tablet that has the modern service, the reader binds `com.onepeloton.affernetservice` with an Intent whose action is `com.onepeloton.affernetservice.IV1Interface`; the service is exported with no permission, and three working apps declare no Peloton permission at all ([PelotonBikeDataSource.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/core/sensor/PelotonBikeDataSource.kt), [OpenRide SENSOR_PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/SENSOR_PROTOCOL.md)). It registers a callback (transaction 1, with any non-null client tag) and receives `BikeData` parcels whose **first six fields are all it needs: rpm, power, stepper position, load-cell reading, current resistance and target resistance**. Cadence is already in rpm, power is in hundredths of a watt, and resistance is already 0 to 100; every project reads those leading fields first and ignores or tolerates the remaining fifty-odd, which is what protects against layout changes between service versions ([BikeData.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/com/onepeloton/affernetservice/BikeData.kt), [BikeDataMapping.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/core/sensor/BikeDataMapping.kt), [grupetto BikeCoreData.kt](https://github.com/doudar/grupetto/blob/af9eb90c53/app/src/main/java/com/spop/poverlay/sensor/BikeCoreData.kt#L15-L29)). The bike reports no speed; projects derive it from power with a published curve fit ([PeloMon Part Ib](https://ihaque.org/posts/2020/12/25/pelomon-part-ib-computing-speed/)). Frames arrived at about 1 Hz on the reference bike when that rate was requested and at 5 Hz with an optional 200 ms request (the default rate with no request is not documented), and cadence changes only about once per pedal revolution, so a game should interpolate between samples ([OpenRide SENSOR_PROTOCOL.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/SENSOR_PROTOCOL.md), [docs/GAMES.md](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/docs/GAMES.md)).

The robustness rules were all learned on hardware. OpenRide binds both bike interfaces and keeps whichever delivers the first decoded frame, which removes the need to identify the board; it reports "connected" only once a frame has decoded, treats three silent seconds as disconnected, and rebinds with a backoff from 1 to 30 seconds after a binding dies, because Android never revives one ([AffernetBikeDataSource.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/core/sensor/AffernetBikeDataSource.kt), [ServiceRebinder.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/core/sensor/ServiceRebinder.kt), [OpenRide PR #46](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/pull/46)). The service itself can go silent for hours until the bike is power-cycled, so the app needs a visible "switch the bike off and on" state instead of showing zeros ([OpenRide #56](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/56)). The stream is independent of subscription state and delivers idle frames with no rider and no class ([PelotonBikeDataSource.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/dev/digitalducktape/openride/core/sensor/PelotonBikeDataSource.kt)). If the checks show only the legacy service, the fallback is a Messenger interface on `com.peloton.service.SensorData`; it is the least-tested path, its two public readers disagree on power scaling by a factor of ten, and its values should be validated against Peloton's own Just Ride screen before being trusted ([selalipop grupetto](https://github.com/selalipop/grupetto), [SensorDataProtocol.kt](https://github.com/doudar/grupetto/blob/af9eb90c53/app/src/main/java/com/spop/poverlay/sensor/v1legacy/SensorDataProtocol.kt#L4-L32)).

### The Bike's real risks are drift and timing, not bricking

No bricked tablet appears in any of OpenPelo's 75 issue and pull-request threads or in OpenRide's tracker, and a 2022 report of a bike that seemed dead with a flashing screen was resolved by restoring an overlay permission the owner had removed from Peloton's apps ([OpenPelo issues](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues?q=is%3Aissue), [DC Rainmaker comments](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2020/06/zwift-trainerroad-peloton.html)). The realistic risks are different. A factory reset can move the tablet forward to less permissive software, since it updates at first network contact ([OpenPelo #44](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/44)). Peloton components keep changing under a working install: a system plugin updated itself on OpenRide's reference bike and shrank every app window, and that bike's firmware build changed during the project ([OpenRide #57](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/57)). The updater package names that one owner disabled (`com.onepeloton.OTAService`, `com.onepeloton.bgupdater`, `com.peloton.updater`) come from a single report with no word on durability ([OpenRide #63](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/63)). Disabling `com.peloton.activity` removes Just Ride, and although the sensor binding survived that on one bike, a full ride with it disabled was never confirmed ([OpenRide #63](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/issues/63)). Warranty statements in the community contradict each other and none cites Peloton, so the fair summary is owner's risk ([FitSwitch developer mode](https://fitswitch.io/blogs/learn/peloton-developer-mode), [DC Rainmaker](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2020/06/zwift-trainerroad-peloton.html)). Recovery does not depend on the screen: holding Volume Up and Power from power-off reaches a recovery menu with a factory-reset item, per Peloton's own instructions ([Peloton status page](https://status.onepeloton.com/incidents/118vkqhv2d9v)).

## The Tread must keep Peloton's software in charge of the belt

### Why the Tread app can only watch

The treadmill interface on the sensor service is unauthenticated and mixes harmless getters with motion commands a few numbers apart: transaction 17 reads belt speed and 45 sets it, and a single write of zero to the set-speed call makes the motor board brake hard ([tread research](https://github.com/aaearon/grupetto/blob/327e860630/docs/tread-support-research.md#L354-L367), [TreadController.java](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/94137eebf6/app/src/com/treadcover/TreadController.java#L56-L59)). Worse for a home-screen replacement, the service runs a **five-second watchdog**: if none of four privileged Peloton clients (`com.peloton.activity`, `com.onepeloton.sensorstateindicator`, the keyguard part of `com.onepeloton.systempluginui`, and `com.onepeloton.fwupdateservice`) is registered, it disables the belt and sets the lock flag ([tread research](https://github.com/aaearon/grupetto/blob/327e860630/docs/tread-support-research.md#L369-L378)). That is why the one project that replaced the Tread's home screen and disabled three Peloton packages then had to impersonate Peloton's lock service, store the owner's PIN, and send unlock, enable and set-speed commands itself ([Peloton-Tread AGENTS.md](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/AGENTS.md), [TreadController.java](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/94137eebf6/app/src/com/treadcover/TreadController.java#L353-L392)). A strictly read-only app and a Tread with Peloton's interface disabled are therefore incompatible on current evidence. The family's rule of no belt control translates into a concrete rule: **on the Tread, disable and uninstall nothing**, start and stop the belt from Peloton's own Just Run screen behind Tread Lock, and let the family's app only display numbers.

That rule has a price which should be stated plainly. On an unsubscribed Tread with everything enabled, `com.peloton.activity` draws an activation and subscription message as a secure system overlay that app permissions cannot block, and `com.onepeloton.systempluginui` was force-closing Netflix- and Prime-type apps on the one Tread studied ([Peloton-Tread AGENTS.md](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/AGENTS.md)). The custom home screen and the streaming tiles may consequently work less well on the Tread than on the Bike, and it is possible that the full design cannot coexist with the read-only rule there. The evidence on the more basic question, whether the belt keeps running with a third-party app in front, is in conflict: one owner runs a read-only overlay over Netflix on a stock Tread and reports only that Peloton's own readout disappears after 10 to 15 minutes, while an unsigned review site claims Tread Lock stops the belt as soon as a third-party app is opened ([OpenPelo #46](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/46), [zelpio](https://www.zelpio.com/blog/peloton-tread-review/)). An earlier design of the Tread launcher deliberately drew a non-focusable overlay over Peloton's Just Run screen so that focus stayed with Peloton and the lock state was not disturbed, which is the fallback layout if a full-screen game turns out to interfere ([CoverService.java](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/app/src/com/treadcover/CoverService.java#L36)).

The safety context justifies the caution. Tread Lock is a software feature: a passcode unlocks the belt, it was reported to re-lock after 45 seconds of inactivity, and since August 2021 Peloton has said it and Just Run are available regardless of membership ([Pelo Buddy](https://www.pelobuddy.com/update-just-run-tread-subscription/), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/peloton-tread-owners-no-longer-need-a-subscription-to-j-1847424543)). The safety key, the stop button and person-present detection live in the controller and the sensor service and act independently of any client app, and the Tread launcher's author still requires a pull-the-key test at low speed before trusting any setup ([Peloton-Tread AGENTS.md](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/main/AGENTS.md), [mi.ke/tread](https://mi.ke/tread/)).

### Belt speed and incline from two getters

The lowest-risk reader is the one the overlay project uses: bind the service with the action `com.onepeloton.affernetservice.ITreadInterface` and poll **transaction 17 (current speed) and transaction 18 (current incline)** about twice a second from a background thread. Each call sends only the interface token and returns one integer in tenths, so 32 means 3.2 mph and 35 means 3.5 percent grade; it registers nothing with the service, so it cannot disturb the callback registry or the watchdog, and it was validated on a `PLTN-TTR01` running Android 10 ([AffernetTreadClient.kt](https://github.com/vmudinas/OpenPeloton_Metrics_Overlay/blob/280834eb3a/app/src/main/java/org/openpelo/beltstatusdump/AffernetTreadClient.kt#L52-L121), [grupetto TreadCombinedSensor.kt](https://github.com/doudar/grupetto/blob/af9eb90c53/app/src/main/java/com/spop/poverlay/sensor/tread/TreadCombinedSensor.kt#L13-L23)). Telemetry flows whenever the Tread is powered, with no workout and no unlock, and the "current" values are real belt state while the "target" values are setpoints ([tread research](https://github.com/aaearon/grupetto/blob/327e860630/docs/tread-support-research.md#L100-L108)). The richer alternative registers a callback (transaction 48, plus 83 for process-death cleanup and 49 to unregister) and receives a 76-field `TreadData` parcel about 20 times a second, with current speed and incline at positions 16 and 17 and read-only views of the lock, safety-key and stop states; two independent projects on two service versions agree on that layout ([TreadData.java](https://github.com/doudar/grupetto/blob/af9eb90c53/app/src/main/java/com/spop/poverlay/sensor/TreadData.java#L130-L209), [TreadCallback.java](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/94137eebf6/app/src/com/treadcover/TreadCallback.java#L89-L131)). If the family uses it, the registration cookie must be the app's own package name and never a Peloton one, since the registry trusts that string and a Peloton name could evict a genuine client ([tread research](https://github.com/aaearon/grupetto/blob/327e860630/docs/tread-support-research.md#L153-L163)).

The guard is structural, not a matter of care. grupetto routes every treadmill call through one wrapper that rejects any transaction code outside a fixed allowlist, and in practice it only ever sends 48, 49 and 83 ([TreadCombinedSensor.kt](https://github.com/doudar/grupetto/blob/af9eb90c53/app/src/main/java/com/spop/poverlay/sensor/tread/TreadCombinedSensor.kt#L85-L121)). The family's reader should make the forbidden codes unrepresentable in the same way, with a test that fails if any of **43, 45, 46, 47, 50 to 59, 65, 66, 68 to 81 or 84 to 86** can be produced, and it should treat every unlisted code as forbidden too, because most have no published meaning ([tread research](https://github.com/aaearon/grupetto/blob/327e860630/docs/tread-support-research.md#L354-L367)). Three smaller rules come from field experience: do not call the treadmill's report-rate transaction (47), because it changes the rate Peloton's own interface receives; do not poll while the screen is asleep, because each poll woke the tablet on the Tread launcher's machine; and do not build from the one public treadmill AIDL file, which lists methods alphabetically and would mis-number every call ([tread research](https://github.com/aaearon/grupetto/blob/327e860630/docs/tread-support-research.md#L181-L185), [TreadController.java](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/94137eebf6/app/src/com/treadcover/TreadController.java), [briancollins AIDL](https://github.com/briancollins/tread/blob/203a9666c9/src/main/aidl/ITreadInterface.aidl#L8-L89)).

### A Tread+ changes the answer

If the 32-inch machine is what the family owns, almost none of the above carries over. **No open-source project reads Tread+ sensors as of November 2026.** The platform string is `aurora` and the interface is `IAuroraInterface`, and that name is all that is public: grupetto's Tread support excludes the Tread+, and the Tread launcher's instructions say to stop if the machine is one ([grupetto README](https://github.com/doudar/grupetto/blob/af9eb90c53/README.md#L15-L24), [Peloton-Tread AGENTS.md](https://github.com/MichaelCyger/Peloton-Tread/blob/94137eebf6/AGENTS.md#L55), [tread research](https://github.com/aaearon/grupetto/blob/327e860630/docs/tread-support-research.md#L66-L68)). Sideloading evidence is old and mixed: Android 7.0 was reported on a Tread+ in January 2021, when one owner could not install anything across more than thirty resets until the membership was reactivated; the only walkthrough (December 2022) reached a Micro-USB data port after removing the tablet's rear cover; one owner in February 2025 had ADB installs working but found the on-device "install unknown apps" switch greyed out; another in March 2025 could not install anything on a spare Tread+ screen; and a June 2026 question from a Tread+ owner about a forced PIN screen has no answer ([DC Rainmaker comments](https://www.dcrainmaker.com/2020/06/zwift-trainerroad-peloton.html), [Tread+ gist](https://gist.github.com/wadewegner/1d6d1bdaac7f50ae170e18fb64449a30), [OpenPelo #5](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/5), [OpenPelo #8](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/8), [OpenPelo #47](https://github.com/doudar/Openpelo/issues/47)). The Tread+ is also the machine recalled in May 2021, about 125,000 units, after a child's death and more than 70 reported incidents ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/es/Recalls/2021/Peloton-Recalls-Tread-Plus-Treadmills-After-One-Child-Died-and-More-than-70-Incidents-Reported)). The defensible plan for a Tread+ is the read-only checks, at most an attempt to install the launcher for streaming tiles, and a game mode that runs without live data. Sending any numbered transaction to an undocumented interface on that machine would be guessing, which every published treadmill project refuses to do.

## Conclusion

The durable dependency in this project is not Peloton's cloud or a membership but two things on the tablet that Peloton can change by update: an undocumented local service and the handful of packages that draw over, close or resize other apps. In the tracker history behind this report (January 2025 to November 2026), most regressions on working installs came from the second group: the app killer, the overlay, the shrunken window. The sensor side moved once, when an August 2025 update removed the legacy service on at least one bike and readers had to switch to `IV1Interface`, while the six leading fields of the bike data are the same in every service version the projects examined ([selalipop PR #10](https://github.com/selalipop/grupetto/pull/10), [BikeData.kt](https://github.com/digitalducktape/OpenRide/blob/main/app/src/main/java/com/onepeloton/affernetservice/BikeData.kt)). That argues for an app that treats Peloton's software as weather: it records the build string, platform, sensor-service version and WebView version on a diagnostics screen, refuses treadmill reads on an unverified service version, and degrades to a launcher without live data instead of guessing.

The two machines also want one codebase with two personalities, chosen at runtime from `peloton_platform`: an owner on the Bike, where the app may be home and may switch off Peloton's nagging packages one reversible command at a time, and a guest on the Tread, where Peloton stays in charge of the belt and the app's whole relationship to the machine is two integers read twice a second. The cheapest facts should be bought first. One evening of read-only checks on both tablets settles the minimum Android version, the browser baseline, whether the treadmill is a Tread or a Tread+, and whether the install gate exists; a throwaway probe app that only logs sensor frames is the right second step, and the over-the-air web bundle is worth building early precisely because it moves most future updates off the least-proven part of the system, the on-device installer.
